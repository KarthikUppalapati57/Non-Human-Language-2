This session indicates a primary focus on Play behavior (67.8%), although other activities are also present. The presence of 'ORD' (Oriented behavior, 16.9%) and 'FFR' (Fast Foraging Run, 11.9%) suggests the play is interspersed with periods of observation and quick foraging attempts.

The vocalization profile is dominated by 'MW' (complex whistles, 66.1%) followed by 'ECT' (Echolocation clicks trains, 32.2%). The high proportion of complex whistles alongside play suggests social communication and coordination during the play session. The presence of echolocation clicks, albeit at a lower percentage, may indicate the dolphins are also intermittently using echolocation to perceive their environment, perhaps identifying potential objects of interest during play or briefly scanning for prey in connection with the foraging runs. The very small amount of 'FB' (Feed Buzz) at 1.7% is likely related to the quick foraging attempts, which is in line with the 'FFR' behavior we see in the behavioral portion.

In summary, the dolphin is primarily engaged in playful social interaction, using complex whistles for communication. These playful periods are interspersed with brief periods of orientation and fast foraging runs, during which echolocation and feeding buzzes are used to identify prey.
